## About the Guide

The **AI Operational Readiness & Governance Practitioner Guide** is a practical methodology for discovering, designing, governing, piloting, operating, and improving AI-enabled work.

It addresses a gap between AI governance principles and the operating practices required to maintain accountability as AI systems, workflows, data, models, prompts, retrieval sources, vendors, permissions, and business conditions change.

The methodology applies both to organizations already using AI and to organizations evaluating AI-enabled work for the first time.

Its goal is not more governance documentation. Its goal is **better operational decisions**.

## Practitioner Method

The methodology connects eleven stages:

1. **Discover** — What is happening today?
2. **Prioritize** — Which opportunities deserve attention?
3. **Classify** — What kind of AI capability is required?
4. **Design** — How should the work change?
5. **Govern** — Who decides, intervenes, and remains accountable?
6. **Evidence** — What must be observed, retained, and verified?
7. **Prepare** — Is the operating model ready to test?
8. **Operate** — How will accountability persist after launch?
9. **Change** — What changes require reassessment or reapproval?
10. **Learn** — What evidence justifies scale, redesign, pause, or retirement?
11. **Communicate** — What does leadership need to decide?

The stages form a connected progression rather than a rigid waterfall. Teams may move backward when new evidence, risk, or operating conditions require redesign.

## Relationship to the AI Operational Readiness Kit

The Practitioner Guide is the narrative and methodology layer above the **AI Operational Readiness Kit**.

The Guide explains the operating method and sequence. Supporting practitioner assets are intended to help teams perform the work, including discovery, prioritization, decision-rights, evidence, pilot-readiness, governance-rhythm, change-trigger, and executive-communication tools.

## Future Research and Validation

Version 1.0 establishes the initial operating methodology. Future research and practitioner validation will continue to examine questions including:

- how risk tiering should enter and evolve through the methodology
- how regulatory obligations should map to operating decisions without turning the Guide into a compliance manual
- which evidence requirements should be universal versus consequence-dependent
- what constitutes material change across different AI patterns and levels of autonomy
- how agentic workflows affect authority, runtime control, intervention, and evidence
- which governance capabilities should be centralized versus federated
- which operating metrics reveal governance drift before an incident or control failure
- where automated governance tooling is useful and where human judgment must remain explicit
- which governance properties must remain invariant as systems, execution paths, tools, and handoffs become more dynamic
